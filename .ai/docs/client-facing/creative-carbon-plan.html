How we build the Creative Carbon system, stage by stage

Creative Carbon Composites makes phenolic laminates, tubes, rods and moulded parts at Kanera, Kheda. This plan takes the Dermat India system we already built (its screens, its one-page order that runs from booking to despatch, its stock and accounts) and turns it into Creative Carbon's system. We keep what is generic, remove what is cosmetics-only, and add the plant registers. Every screen copies a paper register we photographed on the audit day.

The four rules every screen follows

From the brief, in the client's own words. When two ideas conflict, these decide.

1The screen looks like the register it replaces. Same columns, same order, same words (Chindi, Kushan, Daylight, Die No., B-stage)."Initially we start in such a way that it looks like a register, and the entry is only as much as they would do on the register."
280% of the value at 20% of the complexity. Fields printed on the forms but left blank (press top/bottom temperature, curing time) are built, switched off by default, and the owner can switch them on.
3One data-entry operator keys the day's registers for the first 3–4 months. Excel upload of every register is a first-release feature.
4The plant runs on a phone hotspot 15 km from town. Light pages, works offline on entry screens, phone first, one office desktop. Floor tablets come in phase 2.

What the 12 register photos settled

CHECKED

Read photo by photo against the brief. Four open questions now have an answer from the registers themselves, and two details in the brief need correcting.

✓The 2/24 notation is "sheets of this thickness / total sheets in the batch". Every batch on the page adds up: F/01 is 10 + 4 + 4 = 18, F/02 is 10 + 8 = 18, F/03 is 10 + 6 + 6 + 2 = 24, F/04 is 10 + 1 + 6 + 1 = 18. The system shows the total itself; nobody types it.IMG_7787
✓10 ± 10 on the thickness register is the weave, not a tolerance. It reads 10×10 and the readings under it are 25.5–25.8 mm, so it cannot mean 10 mm. The target thickness comes from the batch line; the tolerance needs its own number from the client.IMG_7777
✓Despatch weighment records every sheet. 10-16 means 16 sheets of 10 mm, and there are exactly 16 weights on those rows, written five per line. 12-10 has 10 weights, 15-5 has 5. The screen takes one weight per sheet and totals each thickness.IMG_7776
✓One daylight can hold two sheets. On press 2, batch F/3/10/2026, daylights 4 to 9 each carry a 15 mm and a 10 mm sheet side by side with separate loading weights. The press batch is batch → daylight → sheets, not one thickness per daylight.IMG_7785
!The FG inspection "Quantity KGS" column holds sheet counts. Values are 1, 2, 6, 4, 20, 18 against 8×4 sheets of 25–60 mm, which cannot be kilograms. We record pieces and work out the weight.IMG_7778
!The FG form's "Reason for rejection" header is already crossed out and replaced by hand with ALLOCATION. Entries are export, stock, BHEL, R.B, D.R and a customer name in Gujarati. So the column says who the sheet is set aside for. It becomes "Allocated to" (customer, export or stock), with rejection as a separate field.IMG_7778
✓The press heating slip is partly filled in practice (cycle 4, load 20, press 1, 12.7 mm, CBT max 165, total time 2:25). It stays optional and collapsed, but it is used sometimes.IMG_7788
✓The dryer QC report's Remarks column holds slot weights (637.000, 286.400…), and the ringed figures are day totals of raw cloth and coated pieces (861, 786, 1570). The screen computes those totals. Someone pencilled "Speed" over the RC/VC area and "Before treating" and "After coating" over the weight columns; Kushan is still unexplained.IMG_7783
✓The moulding register adds two operators to the list (Vinod and one more) and notes die thickness in the Die Heat Time row, for example "(34mm)". We add a die thickness field.IMG_7786
✓Seed data comes straight from the photos: chemical balances for Phenol and Methanol in September, 30 reinforcement grades with stock weights as of 29 June, and real batches F/01 to F/04 for 2 Oct.IMG_7759 · 7760 · 7782

Stage 1: Masters

PLANNEDWeek 2

Everything later stages pick from. Seeded from the brief's vocabulary and the photos.

○Item master with eight types. Kilograms everywhere; piece count only on moulded parts. Reinforcement named <brand> <GSM> for paper and by weave for cloth.Seed: 7 chemicals, about 30 reinforcement grades from the 29 June page, resin grades PFC / PFA / PFAC / E-GLASS, laminate grade F2F3.
○Equipment: reactors (CCCPL-VES-1, VES-2…), 3 dryers and the mixer oven, 25 presses (1–20 small, 21–24 big) with daylight count.
○Mould and die master, about 3,000 rows imported from the client's Excel. Dies belong to a customer; plates do not. Die thickness added from the moulding register.
○Loading tolerance by thickness: 25 mm 117.600–118.200, 15 mm 69.300–69.800, 10 mm 45.800–46.300 kg. Warns, never blocks.
○Stores: Warehouse A and B only, no bins. Parties (customer, vendor, transporter, job work) with GSTIN and export flag.
○Department logins for owner, floor supervisor, resin, coating, press / moulding, finished goods and despatch, lab, data entry, accounts (Ahmedabad) and sales. Only the owner sees costs.

Done when: the mould list and item master are in the system, and each department login sees only its screens.

Stage 2: Bulk upload centre

PLANNEDWeek 2, used by every later stage

The data-entry operator's main screen. Built once as a framework, then each register plugs in as it is built.

○One tile per register. Download an Excel template whose columns match the paper register in the same order, with today's date filled in.
○Upload CSV or Excel and see a preview first: rows found, errors, and what will be created. Nothing is saved until you confirm.
○Good rows go in; bad rows come back as a file with a reason column. Uploading the same file twice never double-posts.

Stage 3: Resin plant

PLANNEDWeek 3

Phenol formaldehyde resin batch report CCCPL/F/QC/03, one per batch, entered next day.

○Resin batch entry, field for field: vessel, batch no. CCCPL/DDMMYY/NN, grade, materials in kg, the process steps with temperature and time, tests (pH, gel time, viscosity, solid content), resin yield, chemist and in-charge sign-off.IMG_7758
○Posting the batch takes the chemicals out of stock and puts the resin in, so the chemical book is never written twice. Yield % is worked out and trended (sample batch: 1600 ÷ 2971.5 = 53.8%).
○A failed batch writes off all its input as scrap. No rework path.
○Chemical register as a printable report in the book's own columns (Dt., O.B., Received, Total, Use, Balance), one page per chemical per month. Phenol adds Water and Resin.IMG_7759 · 7760

Done when: a resin batch posts, the chemical pages update by themselves, and the Phenol page matches September's book.

Stage 4: Coating and the B-stage board

PLANNEDWeek 3
○Dryer QC report, one tab per dryer: cloth name, GSM, Kushan, treated and raw cloth weight, balance raw cloth, coated pieces, resin batch, RC %, VC %, two-hourly time slot, DBP, oleic acid, remarks. Day totals worked out.IMG_7783
○This is the only place resin is used against a batch, so each row links to its resin batch. RC and VC outside their range warn.
○Each coating run makes a B-stage lot with a 7-day clock (usable to 10). The B-stage board lists lots by age: amber at day 5, red when expired, with use, sell and scrap actions. Oldest first by default; picking out of order asks for a reason.

PLANNEDWeek 4

○Press loading register for the same batch: press, daylights, sheets per daylight with thickness, grade and loading weight, checked against the tolerance table.IMG_7785

Stage 6: Quality and finished goods

PLANNEDWeek 4
○Thickness inspection: 12 readings per board (rows 4′, 8′, 4′, 8′ by three), target from the batch line. All 12 are needed to clear a board.IMG_7777
○FG inspection test report CCCPL/F/QC/04: batch, item, sheet size, pieces, thickness, allocated to (required), rejection reason (optional).IMG_7778
○Trim loss recorded as weight: expected 7–8% on sheets, 5% on moulded parts, warns when 2 points off.
○Bought-in finished goods enter stock without a batch. Lab test is a file upload per lot and customer, with copy-from-last-time.

Stage 7: Stock, owner dashboard and the demo

PLANNEDWeek 5 · mid-November demo
○One stock screen for all item types, weight first, filtered by type and warehouse, with the movement history behind every balance (Dermat's stock pages and ledger, relabelled).
○Owner dashboard with his three questions and nothing else: what was produced today, is it on plan, any breakdown.

Stage 8: One-page order, booking to despatch

PLANNEDWeeks 6–7

Dermat's order page, stage pages, department queues, history and order book stay as they are. Only the stages change. Production is not a stage, because laminates are made to stock; the order allocates finished lots instead.

Order booked→Advance / LC→Stock allocation→QC & test report→Packing & weighment→Invoice & documents→Despatch
○Order lines by grade, weave, sheet size and thickness, in kg (pieces for moulded parts), with die number on moulded lines. Export orders carry incoterm, port (Mundra, Kandla, Nhava Sheva) and country.
○Stock allocation picks finished lots by thickness and size. If there isn't enough, the shortfall is shown to production.
○Packing records one weight per sheet, as the despatch book does, and totals each thickness. Pallet for export, PP wrap with LDP stitch for local.IMG_7776
○Despatch takes stock out by lot and can never send more than is in stock. Container number, vehicle, LR, e-way bill and IRN are written on the order.

Stage 9: CRM, quotation and purchase

PLANNEDWeeks 7–8
○Enquiries from IndiaMART, WhatsApp, email, phone, walk-in and referral, each with an owner and a next action. Overdue ones go to the owner's dashboard.
○Quotation in INR or export currency, converts to an order without re-typing.
○Purchase order and goods receipt by weight with the supplier's lot, driven by reorder level (Dermat's purchase screens, trimmed).

Stage 10: Documents and Tally

PLANNEDWeek 8
○Invoice, packing list, proforma and e-way bill made from the despatch (Dermat's print layouts with Creative Carbon's GSTIN 24AAICC5600P1ZJ).
○Sales invoices, purchase bills and job work sent one way to Tally. Every send is logged; failures retry and are never dropped. Ledger mapping set with their accountant on the second visit.

Stage 11: Migration, parallel run, go-live

PLANNEDWeeks 9–12

○Choose cloud or a server at the plant from the signal survey. This decision is due in week 1 because it changes how we deploy.

The short version

We start from the Dermat system because its order page, stock, purchase and accounts already work and already look the way we want. Stage 0 strips out the cosmetics parts and Dermat's data. Stages 1 to 7 add the plant registers, from resin to finished sheet, in time for the mid-November demo. Stages 8 to 11 rebuild the order page for laminate sales, link Tally, and take the plant live before year-end.
